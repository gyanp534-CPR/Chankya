# Failure Taxonomy v1

This taxonomy captures *why* students fail a question (thinking pattern), not just what they didn't know.

## Categories

1. **Concept Confusion**  
   Mixing adjacent concepts or misclassifying categories.

2. **Statement Distortion**  
   Misreading or over-trusting statement framing in multi-statement questions.

3. **Partial Correctness Trap**  
   Selecting an option that is mostly correct but has one subtle error.

4. **Surface Matching**  
   Picking answers that match keywords without inferential depth.

5. **Overthinking**  
   Adding assumptions not in the question/passage.

6. **Unstructured Approach**  
   Failing to use a systematic method (especially in logic/arrangement puzzles).

7. **Misreading**  
   Missing qualifiers, conditions, or constraints in the stem.

8. **Calculation Error**  
   Arithmetic or computational mistake after correct setup.

9. **Step Skipping**  
   Skipping foundational steps (e.g., fixing positions first) and losing structure.

10. **Incomplete Inference**  
    Failing to derive the full logical implication from given conditions.

11. **Time Pressure Guessing**  
   Random or low-confidence choice due to time constraints.

12. **Knowledge Gap**  
    Genuine lack of factual or conceptual knowledge.

## Usage

- Use **one** primary `error_prone_area` per question.
- Prefer the *dominant* failure pattern for an average aspirant.
- If truly ambiguous, set `confidence_level = Low`.

## Examples

- Statement-based GS1: `Statement Distortion` or `Concept Confusion`
- Bitcoin-type question: `Partial Correctness Trap`
- CSAT RC: `Surface Matching` or `Overthinking`
- CSAT Arrangement: `Unstructured Approach`
- CSAT Quant with hidden condition: `Misreading`
